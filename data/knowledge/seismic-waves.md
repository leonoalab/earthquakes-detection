# Seismic waves and ground motion

Fault rupture releases energy that travels as seismic waves. Seismometers record the resulting
ground motion. A station record depends on the earthquake source, the path travelled by the waves
and local site conditions.

P waves are compressional waves and normally arrive first. They travel through solids and fluids.
S waves arrive later, produce shear motion and do not travel through fluids. Differences in arrival
times help estimate distance; combining observations from several stations helps locate the event.

Surface waves travel near the Earth's surface and can produce long-duration motion. Soft sediments
can amplify some frequencies compared with rock sites. The summary feed does not contain full
waveforms and cannot predict the response of a particular building.
