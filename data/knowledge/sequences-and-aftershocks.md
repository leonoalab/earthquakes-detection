# Earthquake sequences, aftershocks, foreshocks and swarms

Mainshock, aftershock and foreshock describe relationships inside an earthquake sequence. The
mainshock is the largest event identified in that sequence. Aftershocks occur in the same general
region after it and normally become less frequent with time, although a sequence can last weeks,
months or longer.

The mainshock changes the stress distribution on the ruptured fault and nearby rocks and faults.
Those areas adjust toward a new equilibrium, and some adjustments release energy as smaller
earthquakes. Most aftershocks occur near the main rupture, but sufficient stress change can also
trigger nearby faults. This probabilistic explanation cannot predict each aftershock exactly.

An event can only be called a foreshock after a related larger earthquake occurs. A swarm is a
concentration of events without one clearly dominant mainshock. Proximity in a feed does not prove
causation; classification requires additional geological and seismological evidence.
