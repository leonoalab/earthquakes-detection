# How to interpret earthquake magnitude

Magnitude represents earthquake size at its source. Modern magnitude scales are logarithmic: an
increase of one unit corresponds to about ten times the measured wave amplitude and approximately
32 times more released energy. A magnitude 6 event is therefore not merely a little larger than a
magnitude 5 event.

Magnitude alone does not determine damage. Depth, distance to populated areas, local soil,
construction quality and shaking duration also matter. Two events with similar magnitudes can have
very different consequences.

Do not call every magnitude value a "Richter scale" measurement. USGS can publish different
magnitude types, and the summary feed used by this application does not always provide that detail.

Official source: USGS Earthquake Magnitude, Energy Release, and Shaking Intensity.
https://www.usgs.gov/programs/earthquake-hazards/earthquake-magnitude-energy-release-and-shaking-intensity
